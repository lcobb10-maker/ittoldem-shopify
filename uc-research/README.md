## Research notes

### UC-ENC-AI-0001 — How can computers find answers without repeating expensive work?

An internal Universe City retrieval test recorded **100 passing checks out of 100** against a 130-item test corpus. One reported in-memory baseline stage had a median of **0.5101 milliseconds**, compared with **0.0115 milliseconds** for an adaptive category stage. These stages are not interchangeable end-to-end benchmarks. The report is a useful starting point for teaching indexing, caching, and fair benchmarking, **not** independent proof of a general speedup.

### UC-ENC-AI-0002 — How can fast retrieval also respect permissions?

A separate internal HTTPS acceptance report recorded **30 of 30 checks passing**, including authenticated lookups and checks that raw embeddings were not returned. Selected HTTP lookups were reported in the **32.6–48.5 millisecond** range. This is a different measurement environment from in-memory retrieval.

### UC-ENC-ARCH-0001 — Can a city be built from reusable rules?

Eight procedural architecture scenes passed their internal validity checks. The tests used **headless Chromium with SwiftShader**, not a physical iPad. Several scenes reported **7–9 frames per second**, demonstrating why a successful scene-generation test is not the same as a smooth playable experience.

### UC-ENC-AI-0003 — Can an AI explain where its answer came from?

A grounded-answer and resident-privacy test report recorded **12 of 12 acceptance checks passing**, including source citations and controls for private source histories. Independent privacy and security audits remain outstanding.
